Internal education

Learn to design a chip.

You work through these courses at your own pace, on the same open-source tools that the team uses for its own tapeouts, and you can start with no background in chip design. 12 courses are open now.

Install git and Docker, then set up the workbench: a Linux desktop that runs in a browser tab on your laptop and has the chip design tools and the SKY130 PDK installed.

For both tracks

Take IC102 before your first push to one of the team's Tiny Tapeout projects. It covers the work around the design: making the repository from Tiny Tapeout's template, reading failed runs, reviewing pull requests and finding the commit that broke something. IC103 is for anyone who will bring up or demonstrate a team chip.

The shared workbench

The workbench has XSchem, ngspice, Magic, KLayout, Yosys and LibreLane, among other tools. Every open course uses it except DD101, which runs in your browser, and IC103, which uses KiCad on your own computer.
